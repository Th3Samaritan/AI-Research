Density Functional Theory,
from first principles to AI

You're a materials engineer. Every number you've ever looked up — a modulus, a band gap, a phase diagram — is set by electrons obeying quantum mechanics. The exact equations are unsolvable for real materials. DFT is the 1998-Nobel-winning workaround that made electrons computable, and it now underpins ~40,000 papers a year and every major materials database. This course takes you from "what is a wavefunction?" to running machine-learned DFT surrogates — with code you run yourself at every step.

00

Why DFT exists

One sentence per era: quantum mechanics (1926) gave us the exact equation for matter; it proved unsolvable beyond a few electrons; DFT (1964–65) replaced the unsolvable object with a solvable one — the electron density — at the price of one unknown term we approximate; computers made it routine; and AI (2020s) is now learning from DFT's outputs to go 10,000× faster.

The object swap that defines the field

Exact quantum mechanics tracks the many-electron wavefunction \(\Psi(\mathbf{r}_1,\dots,\mathbf{r}_N)\) — a function of \(3N\) coordinates. DFT tracks the density \(n(\mathbf{r})\) — a function of 3 coordinates, no matter how many electrons. Everything in this course is the story of why that swap is legal and what it costs.

$$\Psi(\mathbf{r}_1,\dots,\mathbf{r}_N)\;\longrightarrow\;n(\mathbf{r})$$
01

The quantum minimum

Three ideas carry the whole course: (1) electrons are described by a wavefunction whose square is a probability; (2) confinement makes energy come in discrete levels; (3) on a computer, finding those levels is a matrix eigenvalue problem — the same one, from this toy box all the way up to VASP.

Interactive

Particle in a box — squeeze it and watch the levels

The Schrödinger equation \( -\tfrac{1}{2}\psi'' + V\psi = E\psi \) in a box of width \(L\) allows only \(E_n = \dfrac{n^2\pi^2}{2L^2}\). Drag the width: smaller box ⇒ higher, more widely spaced levels. This is why nano-materials have different colors and gaps than bulk — quantum confinement is real engineering.

Hydrogen & the variational principle

Hydrogen is the exactly solvable calibration atom: \(E_n = -\tfrac{1}{2n^2}\) hartree, with s, p, d states degenerate — an "accident" of the pure \(-1/r\) potential. Screening in many-electron atoms breaks the accident and orders the periodic table. The variational principle then gives us a rigorous strategy for everything harder than hydrogen:

$$E[\psi_{\text{trial}}]\;\ge\;E_{\text{exact}}\quad\text{— guesses can only err upward.}$$

Expand the guess in a basis set (Gaussians in chemistry, plane waves in solids) and minimizing energy becomes solving \(H\mathbf{c}=ES\mathbf{c}\). More basis functions ⇒ lower ⇒ better, never below truth.

The Wall — why exact QM dies

The many-electron wavefunction lives in \(3N\) dimensions. Storing it on even a pathetic 10-point-per-axis grid needs \(10^{3N}\) numbers. Slide N and watch the universe run out of atoms.

03

DFT — the two miracles

Hohenberg–Kohn (1964): the ground-state density determines everything — energy, structure, all of it. Kohn–Sham (1965): you can get the true density from a fictitious set of non-interacting electrons feeling an effective potential. All remaining many-body hardness is quarantined into one term.

The Kohn–Sham equations

$$\Big[-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla^2 + \underbrace{v_{\text{ext}}(\mathbf r)}_{\text{nuclei}} + \underbrace{v_{H}[n](\mathbf r)}_{\text{cloud repulsion}} + \underbrace{v_{xc}[n](\mathbf r)}_{\text{everything else}}\Big]\,\varphi_i = \varepsilon_i\,\varphi_i, \qquad n(\mathbf r)=\sum_i^{occ}|\varphi_i|^2$$

The potential depends on the density; the density comes from solving with that potential. Chicken and egg ⇒ iterate to self-consistency. DFT in one line: Kohn–Sham equations + SCF loop + an approximation for \(v_{xc}\).

Interactive

The SCF loop — and why "mixing" exists

Each iteration you blend new density into old: \(n \leftarrow (1-\beta)\,n + \beta\, n_{\text{new}}\). Step the loop at different mixing β. Small β: slow but safe. β near 1: fast. Push β past 1 (over-shooting): oscillation — and beyond 2, divergence. Real codes expose this knob (mixing_beta in Quantum ESPRESSO) and yes, real calculations die from it.

04

The xc zoo — Jacob's ladder

All of DFT's error hides in \(v_{xc}\). Approximations are ranked on "Jacob's ladder" — each rung uses more information about the density, costs more, and is (usually) more accurate. Click the rungs.

LDA — Local Density Approximation1965 · cheapest
Pretend each point of the material is a uniform electron gas of that density. Absurdly simple, weirdly effective for solids. Overbinds: lattice constants ~1–2% too small, atomization energies poor. Still the base layer of everything.
GGA — e.g. PBE1996 · the workhorse
Adds the density's gradient. PBE is the default of computational materials science and of the Materials Project. Good geometries and formation energies; still underestimates band gaps by ~40–50%.
meta-GGA — e.g. SCAN2015
Adds kinetic-energy density. Notably better formation energies and diversely bonded systems; moderate extra cost. Increasingly the new default for materials databases (Materials Project now runs r²SCAN).
Hybrids — e.g. HSE0610–100× cost
Mix in a fraction of exact Hartree–Fock exchange. Band gaps become respectable (Si: ~1.2 eV vs experiment 1.17). The standard for semiconductor gaps — at a painful price in plane-wave codes.
Beyond — RPA, GW, ML functionalsfrontier
Many-body perturbation theory (GW) for true quasiparticle gaps; and machine-learned functionals (DeepMind's DM21, 2021) — a neural network as \(v_{xc}\) — the first place AI entered DFT's inner machinery.

The one failure to memorize: LDA/PBE underestimate band gaps (Si: ≈0.6 eV predicted vs 1.17 eV real) — a structural issue (the "derivative discontinuity"), not sloppiness. Every band-structure paper you read will dance around it.

05

Crystals & bands

A crystal is a periodic potential. Bloch's theorem labels states by \(k\) in the Brillouin zone; the natural basis is plane waves — exactly what VASP and Quantum ESPRESSO use. Levels smear into bands; gaps open at the zone boundary; filling decides metal vs semiconductor.

Interactive

Watch a band gap open

Bands of \(V(x)=V_0\cos(2\pi x/a)\), computed live in your browser by diagonalizing the plane-wave Hamiltonian (7 plane waves, Jacobi eigensolver). At \(V_0=0\): folded free-electron parabola, no gap — metal-like. Turn up the crystal potential and a gap of width ≈ \(V_0\) opens at the zone edge.

gap at zone edge: 0.00

Filling rule (2 electrons per k-state): 1 valence electron per cell ⇒ half-filled band ⇒ metal (Na). Even count filling bands completely, with a gap above ⇒ semiconductor/insulator (Si, diamond). One counting rule, whole material classes.

06

Real calculations

Production DFT adds three practicalities: a plane-wave cutoff (basis size — converge it), a k-point mesh (Brillouin-zone sampling — converge it), and pseudopotentials (replace inert core electrons with an effective potential so the basis stays sane). The workflow around the physics is Python: ASE.

07

AI4DFT

DFT's accuracy made it the data factory: millions of computed structures in the Materials Project, OQMD, AFLOW. Graph neural networks trained on that data (CHGNet, MACE, M3GNet) now predict energies and forces at DFT accuracy in milliseconds — unlocking long dynamics and discovery at the scale of GNoME's 2.2 million candidate crystals.

The fine print, which is the actual lesson: an MLIP is an interpolation of its training set. Off-distribution — weird stoichiometries, extreme pressure, broken bonds — it can fail silently. Professional practice: spot-check against real DFT, always. AI didn't replace DFT; it made DFT the ground truth everything else is trained on.
