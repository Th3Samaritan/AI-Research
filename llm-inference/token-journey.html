What is real and what is a stand-in

The structure is real: layer count and pattern, head counts and grouping, norms, cache layout, router, LM head. So are the shapes and costs: they come from each model's official config. The numbers flowing through (vector bars, scores, states) come from a 16-dimensional toy with the same wiring and random weights, so you can see every value. Its next word is noise; the math path is identical.

Where the time goes for this token

real model, ideal bandwidth

The highlighted segment is the step now playing. In decode, almost every step is memory-bound: the GPU waits for weights and cache to arrive. Switch to prefill and the big matrix steps become compute-bound, because each weight is reused for every prompt token. Raise the context and watch the teal cache segment grow. 2 · Activations in 3D

The FFN's switch, as a surface

A gated FFN neuron has two inputs, gate and up, so its activation is a surface, not a curve. Gold dots are the 32 toy neurons of the token you are following, at the magnified layer. Changing the function here changes it in the journey too.

Drag to orbit

        

        
How to read it. Along the gate axis the surface follows the activation's shape. Along the up axis it's a straight ramp, because up just scales the result. So the gate decides whether a neuron fires and up decides what it passes on. Turn on the gradient view with ReLU or ReGLU: the flat zero region is where neurons stop learning.

Things to try
  • Narrow valley, no noise: SGD zig-zags across the walls while Adam and RMSProp head straight down the floor.
  • Saddle point: SGD barely moves off the ridge, while momentum and Adam escape.
  • Set λ = 0.3: Adam with L2 and AdamW stop in different places. Both are pulled toward the origin, but AdamW's pull isn't rescaled by √v̂.
  • Raise the learning rate until SGD diverges. Then switch to warmup + cosine: small early steps, then decay so it settles in the minimum instead of bouncing around it.

  






  4 · Train a tiny model
  
A real training run in your browser

  
This is a genuine next-word model: two previous words → embedding → 48 hidden neurons → softmax over the vocabulary. It's trained from scratch on 24 sentences with real backpropagation. All five optimizers start from the same weights and see the same batches. Change the activation, weight decay or schedule and see what learns.

  

    

      
      
      
      
      
      
 

    

    
    
    

    
Each optimizer gets its usual base learning rate (SGD 0.3, momentum 0.05, RMSProp 0.003, Adam/AdamW 0.006), and the slider scales them all together. Choose ReLU with a high multiplier to see neurons die: they get pushed below zero, their gradient becomes 0, and they never recover. That's one reason modern LLMs use SiLU or GELU.
